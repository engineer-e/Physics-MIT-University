Step-by-Step Computational & Mathematical Algorithm

1. The Coordinate Transformation

To compute circular coordinates, we define a radial unit vector $\hat{r}$ and a tangential unit vector $\hat{\theta}$: $$\hat{r} = \cos\theta\hat{i} + \sin\theta\hat{j}$$ $$\hat{\theta} = -\sin\theta\hat{i} + \cos\theta\hat{j}$$ Because these coordinate axes rotate with the particle, taking the derivative yields: $$\frac{d\hat{r}}{dt} = \frac{d\theta}{dt}(-\sin\theta\hat{i} + \cos\theta\hat{j}) = \frac{d\theta}{dt}\hat{\theta}$$

2. Velocity Derivation

The position is defined as $\vec{r} = r\hat{r}$. Assuming constant radius $r$, the velocity is: $$\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = r\frac{d\hat{r}}{dt} = r\frac{d\theta}{dt}\hat{\theta}$$ Substituting $\omega_z = \frac{d\theta}{dt}$, we write: $$\vec{v} = r\omega_z\hat{\theta}$$

3. The 3D Cross Product Vector Definition

Angular velocity is a pseudovector $\vec{\omega} = \omega_z\hat{k}$. The tangential velocity can be computed in 3D using the vector cross product: $$\vec{v} = \vec{\omega} \times \vec{r}$$

Course Syllabus & Intuition

Let's build a solid mental model step-by-step

1

The "Perspective" Paradox

An observer looking from above a rotating plate sees a counterclockwise circle. An observer looking from below the plate sees a clockwise circle! Words like "clockwise" depend entirely on your vantage point. We need a vector that remains invariant under perspective changes.

2

The Normal Unit Vector $\hat{n}$

Any flat plane is uniquely defined by a vector perpendicular to it (the normal vector). By defining our coordinate axis along the plane normal, we can resolve orientation unambiguously.

3

The Right-Hand Rule

How do we match rotational direction to a physical axis?

  • Curl fingers of your right hand along the orbital velocity $\vec{v}$.
  • Your thumb naturally points in the direction of the angular velocity vector $\vec{\omega}$.
