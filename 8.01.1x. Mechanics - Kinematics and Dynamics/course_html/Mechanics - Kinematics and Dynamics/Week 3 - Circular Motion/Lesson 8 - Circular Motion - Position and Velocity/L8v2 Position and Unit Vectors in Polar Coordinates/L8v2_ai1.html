Academic Concept & Critical Thinking Check

The Core Shift (Static vs. Dynamic)

Cartesian unit vectors &hat;i and &hat;j are globally fixed across space. In polar coordinates, &hat;r and &hat;θ are local unit vectors that rotate as the position vector moves. Look at the alignment visualizer as you drag the point to see this vector rotation in action.

Singularities & Critical Question

Drag the point P exactly to the origin O ($r = 0$). What happens to the direction of &hat;r and &hat;θ? They become singular—the coordinate angle $\theta$ is completely undefined. Physics equations in polar form must be handled carefully at $r = 0$!

Unit Vector Projections Explained

A vector is defined solely by its magnitude and direction. This allows us to translate &hat;r and &hat;θ directly to the origin to analyze their relationship with &hat;i and &hat;j.

In this aligned state:

  • &hat;r makes an angle θ directly with the x-axis. Its horizontal component is $\cos\theta$ and vertical is $\sin\theta$.
  • &hat;θ is rotated $90^\circ$ counterclockwise. Looking closely at its projections, you'll see the horizontal component points in the negative x-direction ($-\sin\theta$), while its vertical component points upwards ($\cos\theta$).
