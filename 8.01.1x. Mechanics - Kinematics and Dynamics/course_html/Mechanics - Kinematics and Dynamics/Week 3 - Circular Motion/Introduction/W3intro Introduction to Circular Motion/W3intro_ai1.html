Coordinate Intuition

Why is Cartesian ($x, y$) so complex for circular constraints, and why does the polar coordinate frame ($r, \theta$) yield flawless elegance? Let's unpack the vector mechanics.

1. Position Vector Mastery

In circular motion, the distance $r$ from the origin is strictly constrained.

// Cartesian Coordinates:
$x(t) = r\cos(\theta(t)), \quad y(t) = r\sin(\theta(t))$
// Polar Coordinates:
$\vec{r}(t) = r \hat{r}$

By aligning our coordinate vector $\hat{r}$ directly with the path's radius, the algebraic representation simplifies from complex sinusoidal functions into a single static radial vector.

2. Velocity Vector & Moving Unit Basis

Taking the time-derivative of position, the polar unit vectors $\hat{r}$ and $\hat{\theta}$ rotate as time evolves:

$\frac{d\hat{r}}{dt} = \omega \hat{\theta}$
$\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = r \omega \hat{\theta} \quad (v = r \omega)$

Velocity points exclusively along the tangential direction ($\hat{\theta}$). No radial component ($v_r = 0$) can exist if the object respects its constraint.

3. Double Acceleration Phenomenon

Taking another derivative, we uncover two distinct components of acceleration:

$\vec{a} = a_r\hat{r} + a_\theta\hat{\theta}$
$a_r = -r\omega^2 = -\frac{v^2}{r} \quad \text{(Centripetal)}$
$a_\theta = r\alpha = r\frac{d\omega}{dt} \quad \text{(Tangential)}$

Centripetal ($a_c$): Pulls inward. Always present for any curved motion to continuously bend the velocity vector.
Tangential ($a_t$): Acts along the speed path. Only exists when the object is speeding up or slowing down ($\alpha \neq 0$).

Newton's Second Law

Forces do not "want" to make elements spin. For an object to turn, some physical force (tension, normal force, gravity) must act as the constrained centripetal mediator.

Forces in Constrained Motion

RADIAL FORCE ($F_r$) $m \frac{v^2}{r}$ Pulls inward. Maintains structural circle constraint.
TANGENTIAL FORCE ($F_t$) $m r \alpha$ Drives angular speed up/down along path perimeter.

Expert Mental Model: "Centripetal" is a Job Description

There is no distinct physical force in nature called the "centripetal force." Instead, the term describes a roles-and-responsibilities requirement:

  • String Pendulum: String tension ($T$) acts as the centripetal job fulfiller.
  • Car around a bend: Static friction between rubber and asphalt fulfills the centripetal role.
  • Planet orbit: Gravitational attraction fulfills the centripetal demand.

Critical Thinking Experiment

What happens if the circular constraint suddenly snaps (e.g., cutting the string)? Click "CUT CONSTRAINT" on the live simulator view above. Notice how the particle stops turning instantly and drifts off straight, tangentially. Without radial force, $\vec{a} = 0$, confirming Newton's First Law.

Computational Engine

To compute the motion on your monitor screen, the system uses numerical integration (specifically, the **Euler-Cromer Method**).

System Integration Steps (Per Frame)
1
Evaluate Angular Forces

Identify current angular acceleration ($\alpha$). For constant nonuniform, it is manual. For a gravity pendulum, it updates dynamically:
$\alpha = -\frac{g}{r} \sin(\theta)$

2
Update Angular Velocity ($\omega$)

Integrate acceleration over the small timestep $dt$:
$\omega_{\text{new}} = \omega_{\text{old}} + \alpha \, dt$

3
Update Rotational Position ($\theta$)

Apply integrated velocity to find the new angular state:
$\theta_{\text{new}} = \theta_{\text{old}} + \omega_{\text{new}} \, dt$

4
Translate to Cartesian Coordinates for Canvas Render

To render on your screen, we transform polar states back to coordinates:
$x = x_c + r \cos(\theta), \quad y = y_c - r \sin(\theta)$

Sim Precision Variables:
  • Fixed Integrator Delta: $dt = 0.016\,\text{s}$ (60Hz)
