Dynamic Physics State Sync (Live Highlight)

Uniform Motion \[\alpha = 0\]

Since angular velocity is constant, there is zero tangential acceleration: \(a_\theta = 0\). The particle feels only the inward radial pull \(a_r = -r\omega^2\) that continuously updates direction to maintain a circle.

Speeding Up \[\text{sign}(\omega) = \text{sign}(\alpha)\]

The tangential acceleration \(\vec{a}_\theta\) is parallel to the velocity vector \(\vec{v}\), driving an increase in total kinetic energy. Radial acceleration rapidly surges since \(a_r \propto \omega^2\).

Slowing Down \[\text{sign}(\omega) \neq \text{sign}(\alpha)\]

The tangential acceleration \(\vec{a}_\theta\) is anti-parallel (opposite) to velocity. The particle sheds speed. Note that \(a_r\) remains strictly inward (negative radial coordinate), but shrinks as velocity drops.

Instantaneous Rest \[\omega = 0, \alpha \neq 0\]

A critical conceptual check: When the particle stops momentarily to reverse direction, \(\omega = 0\), rendering the radial acceleration \(a_r = -r\omega^2 = 0\). The entire acceleration vector is momentarily **strictly tangential**!

Exact Polar Vector Derivation

How polar derivatives generate both acceleration vectors naturally via Calculus.

Step 1: Position Vector

In circular polar systems, radius \(r\) is constant:

\[\vec{r} = r\,\hat{r}\]
Step 2: Velocity & Polar Derivative

Differentiating position using the chain rule, where \(\frac{d\hat{r}}{dt} = \frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\hat{\theta}\):

\[\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = r\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\hat{\theta} = r\omega\,\hat{\theta}\]
Step 3: Acceleration & Product Rule

Differentiating velocity \(\vec{v} = r\omega\,\hat{\theta}\) using the mathematical product rule since both \(\omega\) and \(\hat{\theta}\) change over time:

\[\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(r\omega\,\hat{\theta}\right)\]

Applying product rule expands this expression into:

\[\vec{a} = r\,\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}\,\hat{\theta} + r\,\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\frac{d\hat{\theta}}{dt}\]

Substituting the derivative of the tangential unit vector, \(\frac{d\hat{\theta}}{dt} = -\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,\hat{r}\):

\[\vec{a} = \underbrace{r\,\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}\,\hat{\theta}}_{\vec{a}_\theta \text{ (Tangential)}} - \underbrace{r\,\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right)^2\hat{r}}_{\vec{a}_r \text{ (Radial)}}\]
