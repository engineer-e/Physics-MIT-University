01. The 2D Grid Setup

For most problems in physics and vector math, working in two dimensions (2D) is ideal. It allows us to formally map vector movements onto a simple, easily understood grid of perpendicular axes.

Formalization: Grids let us map arbitrary lines to numerical coordinates.
Simplicity: Simplifies complex operations like vector addition and scaling into standard coordinate arithmetic.

02. Cartesian Unit Vectors

Once the axes are configured, we require unit vectors. A unit vector has a magnitude of exactly $1$ and points in a defined, fixed direction.

In standard Cartesian systems, we use:

$\hat{i}$ (i-hat)
Points strictly in the positive $x$ direction. Magnitude = $1$.
$\hat{j}$ (j-hat)
Points strictly in the positive $y$ direction. Magnitude = $1$.

"In Cartesian coordinates, these unit vectors remain identical no matter what point in space you reside at."

03. Arbitrary Unit Vectors

What if we want a unit vector in the direction of an arbitrary vector $\vec{v}$?
