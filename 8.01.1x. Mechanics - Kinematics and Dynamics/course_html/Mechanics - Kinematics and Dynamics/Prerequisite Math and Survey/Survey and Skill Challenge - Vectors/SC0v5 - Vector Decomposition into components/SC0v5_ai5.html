Decomposing Vectors into Orthogonal Components

A complete guide to components, unit vectors, projections, and coordinate independent properties.

1

What is Vector Decomposition?

Decomposing a vector means breaking down a single vector into several components that, when added together sequentially, perfectly reconstruct the original vector. In 2D coordinate geometry, we select orthogonal axes (X and Y directions) as our components.

$$\vec{A} = A_x \hat{i} + A_y \hat{j}$$
2

Projections onto Axes

Projections are essentially the shadow cast by the vector directly onto the coordinate axes. Geometrically, these projections represent the sides of a right triangle where the original vector is the hypotenuse. We calculate these using trigonometric ratios.

$$\begin{aligned} A_x &= |\vec{A}| \cos(\theta) \\ A_y &= |\vec{A}| \sin(\theta) \end{aligned}$$
3

The Power of Unit Vectors

Unit vectors ($\hat{i}$, $\hat{j}$, $\hat{k}$) have a length of exactly one unit. They point strictly along coordinate axes (X, Y, Z). They act as directional indicators; multiplying them by scalar numbers defines vectors of any size.

$|\hat{i}| = 1$ (X-Axis) $|\hat{j}| = 1$ (Y-Axis)
Axiom of Location Independence

Vectors Can Exist Anywhere

A classic point of confusion in physics is believing a vector is tied to a specific location on a cartesian layout. It isn't! A vector represents change in position, velocity, or force. Dragging the tail of the vector across the board shifts its coordinates, but its displacement stays exactly the same.

Coordinate A (Origin):
$\text{Tail } (0, 0) \to \text{Head } (-3, 2)$
$\vec{V} = -3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}$
Coordinate B (Shifted):
$\text{Tail } (2, 1) \to \text{Head } (-1, 3)$
$\vec{V} = -3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}$
Since $(-1 - 2) = -3$ and $(3 - 1) = 2$, these displacements are identical.
