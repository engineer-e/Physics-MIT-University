### 1. Scalar Multiplication (Scaling)
Your explanation is spot on regarding **Vector B**.
*   **Definition**: A vector is indeed defined by its magnitude (length) and direction.
*   **The Process**: When you multiply a vector $\vec{A}$ by a scalar $k$, you are only changing the magnitude. The direction remains unchanged if $k$ is positive, or reverses if $k$ is negative.
    *   In your example, multiplying $\vec{A}$ by $2$ creates $\vec{B}$. Since the result has the same direction but double the length, this is correct.

### 2. Vector Addition (The Parallelogram Rule)
You correctly identified the graphical method: **Head-to-Tail**.
*   To add two vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$:
    1.  Place the tail of $\vec{B}$ at the head of $\vec{A}$.
    2.  Draw a line from the tail of $\vec{A}$ to the head of $\vec{B}$.
*   **Result**: This closing side is the **resultant vector** (often called $\vec{R}$ or $\vec{C}$ in your text).
*   **Alternative Method**: You also mentioned sliding the tail of $\vec{A}$ to the head of $\vec{B}$, which creates a parallelogram. The diagonal starting from the common origin represents the sum. Both methods yield the same result; this is known as the **Parallelogram Law**.

### 3. Subtraction (The "Reverse Addition" Concept)
Your approach to subtraction using scalar multiplication and addition is mathematically sound.
*   Mathematically, $\vec{A} - \vec{B}$ is equivalent to $\vec{A} + (-\vec{B})$.
*   The process you described works perfectly:
    1.  Find the negative of the vector ($-\vec{B}$). This flips the direction of $\vec{B}$ and doubles its length.
    2.  Add $\vec{A}$ and $(-\vec{B})$ using the head-to-tail rule.
*   **Geometric Interpretation**: If you imagine a triangle formed by placing $\vec{B}$, then adding $-\vec{B}$ brings you back to the starting point, the final vector $\vec{A} + (-\vec{B})$ connects the start of $\vec{A}$ directly to the end of $-\vec{B}$.
