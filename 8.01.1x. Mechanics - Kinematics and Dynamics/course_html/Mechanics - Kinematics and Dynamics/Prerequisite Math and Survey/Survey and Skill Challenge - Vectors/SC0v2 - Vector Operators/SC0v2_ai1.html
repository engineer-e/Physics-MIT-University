01. What is a Vector?

A vector is fully defined by its magnitude (length) and direction. In the graphic, Vector A and Vector B are displayed.

Try dragging Vector B around the canvas by its tail. Notice that its characteristics do not change! A vector is identical anywhere in space, including when moved to the origin.

Translate vectors by grabbing near their tails. Modify their lengths/angles by dragging their heads.

02. Scalar Multiplication

Multiplying a vector by a scalar (a simple number) rescales its magnitude.

Let's visualize scalar scaling:

Notice: Positive values scale length in the same direction. Negative values reverse the direction.

03. Vector Addition: Tail-to-Head

To add vectors graphically, slide the tail of Vector B to the head of Vector A.

Their sum, Vector C, is drawn directly from the tail of A to the head of the translated B.

04. Addition: Parallelogram Rule

You can also add them by sliding the tail of A to the head of B. This demonstrates that addition is commutative: A + B = B + A.

This forms a parallelogram. The resultant sum Vector C is the main diagonal.

05. Vector Subtraction

Subtraction is just adding a negative vector:
C = A - B 👉 C = A + (-1 × B).

We multiply B by -1 to get -B, and then add A to -B head-to-tail.
