Theoretical Foundation

What is the difference?

Physical quantities are split into two categories based on whether direction matters. Explore the cards below to see how scalars only need a size, while vectors require a spatial heading.

Magnitude Only

Scalars

A scalar is a quantity fully described by a single numerical value (its magnitude) and a unit. It has no direction. Changing its direction has no physical meaning.

Example 1: Temperature

A thermometer reading of 72°F has magnitude, but pointing the thermometer North doesn't change the heat.

Example 2: Length / Mass

An object with a length of 5 meters is 5 meters regardless of which way it is oriented.

Magnitude + Direction

Vectors

A vector is a physical object that possesses both a magnitude and a direction in space. To fully specify it, you must tell both how much, and where it's pointing.

Example 1: Force (Pull)

Pulling a rope with 50 Newtons of force. The direction of the pull dictates which way the object slides.

Example 2: Wind Velocity

Wind blowing at 15 mph Southwest. Knowing the direction is critical for predicting weather movements.

Quick Lesson

You cannot pull the box with just "50 Newtons." You must pull in a specific direction. Thus, Force and Velocity are Vectors. The box's mass is always the same no matter which direction you face, so Mass is a Scalar.
