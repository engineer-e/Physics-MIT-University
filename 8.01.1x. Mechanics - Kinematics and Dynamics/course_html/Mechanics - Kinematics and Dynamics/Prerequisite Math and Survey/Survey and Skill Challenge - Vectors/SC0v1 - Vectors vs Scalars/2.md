Here are the requested elements for the topic **"Distinguishing Vectors and Scalars."**

### ⚡ Cues (Key Takeaways)
*   **Scalars**: Described by a single number (Magnitude Only). No direction involved.
    *   *Example*: Temperature, Length, Speed.
*   **Vectors**: Described by a magnitude AND a specific direction (often visualized as an arrow).
    *   *Example*: Force, Velocity, Displacement.
*   **The Test**: Ask yourself, *"Does this quantity have a direction associated with it?"* If yes $\rightarrow$ Vector. If no $\rightarrow$ Scalar.

---

### 📝 Notes (Key Points & Concepts)
*   **Magnitude vs. Direction**: The defining characteristic is the presence or absence of direction.
    *   Scalars possess only magnitude (how much).
    *   Vectors possess both magnitude (how much) and direction (where/which way).
*   **Scalar Fields**: A map where a scalar property (like temperature) varies across space but has no directional component. The wind velocity is a **Vector Field** because it includes direction.
*   **Real-World Analogy**: Imagine drawing on a piece of paper.
    *   Writing "5" is a **Scalar**.
    *   Drawing an arrow pointing East with the label "10 units long" is a **Vector**.

| Feature | Scalar | Vector |
