Core System Definitions & Assumptions

As explained by the MIT expert, analyzing compound pulley assemblies requires modeling multiple subsystems separately while factoring in structural constraints. We observe three distinct physical sub-bodies:

1. Block 1 (Mass m₁)

Hangs freely from Pulley A. Affected by gravity ($m_1g$) and string tension ($T$).

2. Pulley B + Block 2

Movable assembly B. Double-suspended string delivers combined vertical pull of $2T$.

3. Assumptions

Pulley mass $m_p \approx 0$, massless ideal rope, no slip. Yields uniform tension $T$.

The Kinematic Constraint Equations

Since the rope cannot stretch, the coordinate length $L$ of the rope represents a geometric invariant:

$L = y_1 + 2y_2 + \text{constant}$

Taking the first and second time derivatives ($d/dt$) reveals the fundamental structural constraints:

Velocity Limit

$v_1 + 2v_2 = 0$

Accel. Limit

$a_1 + 2a_2 = 0$

💡 Physical meaning: If mass 1 moves downward by 20cm, movable mass 2 must lift upwards by exactly 10cm.

Simultaneous Equations Solution

Formulating Newton's second law ($F = ma$) along the positive downwards direction for each body yields:

Block 1:

$m_1 g - T = m_1 a_1$

Assembly 2 (Combined):

$m_2 g - 2T = m_2 a_2$

By substituting the kinematics acceleration constraint $a_1 = -2a_2$, we isolate the analytical solution formulas utilized by our computer integration engine:

Mass 2 Acceleration ($a_2$):

$a_2 = \frac{m_2 - 2m_1}{4m_1 + m_2} g$

Rope Tension Force ($T$):

$T = \frac{3 m_1 m_2}{4 m_1 + m_2} g$
