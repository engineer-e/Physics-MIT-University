To compute the equations of motion for complex system geometries, we must establish a clear coordinate reference system and use rope length constraints. Hover or tap the interactive boxes below to highlight the variables directly in the physical simulation.

Step 1: Constraint Equation

Let the ceiling be reference coordinate \(y=0\). Mass 1 has position coordinate \(y_1\). The movable pulley supporting mass 2 has position \(y_2\). Assuming rope inextensibility:

\[y_1 + 2y_2 = L \quad (\text{Rope Length Constant})\]

Differentiating twice with respect to time yield acceleration constraint:

\[a_1 + 2a_2 = 0 \implies a_1 = -2a_2\]
Step 2: Free Body Equations (FBD)

Isolating each mass, we apply Newton's 2nd Law (\(\Sigma F = ma\)), paying close attention to tension vector forces:

For Mass 1 \[T_1 - m_1 g = m_1 a_1\]
For Mass 2 & Pulley \[m_2 g - 2T_1 = m_2 a_2\]
Step 3: Analytical Resolution

Using the acceleration constraint \(a_1 = -2a_2\) and substituting Tension \(T_1 = m_1(g - 2a_2)\) into Mass 2's FBD yields the exact systemic accelerations:

\[a_2 = \frac{m_2 - 2m_1}{m_2 + 4m_1}g \quad ; \quad a_1 = -2 \left(\frac{m_2 - 2m_1}{m_2 + 4m_1}\right)g\]

Differential Tension Integration Theoretical Slicing

To resolve internal forces across continuous media, we model individual mass elements as infinitesimally small increments. Consider a vertical rope of length \(L\), total mass \(M\), carrying a bottom point load \(m_0\). Let us isolate a differential chunk \(dy\) at height \(y\) from the bottom end.

Step 1: Linear Mass Density \(\lambda\)

Since the rope is homogeneous, the mass distribution is linear. For a segment of length \(dy\), the infinitesimal mass \(dm\) is:

\[\lambda = \frac{M}{L} \implies dm = \lambda dy = \left(\frac{M}{L}\right) dy\]
Step 2: Differential Equilibrium

An isolated section at height \(y\) undergoes vertical acceleration \(a\). The upper edge experiences Tension \(T(y+dy)\) and the lower edge experiences Tension \(T(y)\).

\[T(y + dy) - T(y) - dm \cdot g = dm \cdot a\] \[dT - \lambda dy \cdot g = \lambda dy \cdot a \implies \frac{dT}{dy} = \lambda(g + a)\]
Step 3: Definite Integration

We integrate from the bottom boundary (\(y=0\), where Tension must balance the dead load \(T(0) = m_0(g+a)\)) up to any arbitrary height \(y\):

\[\int_{T(0)}^{T(y)} dT' = \int_{0}^{y} \lambda(g+a) dy'\] \[T(y) - T(0) = \lambda(g+a)y \implies T(y) = \underbrace{m_0(g+a)}_{\text{Bottom Load Weight}} + \underbrace{\frac{M}{L}y(g+a)}_{\text{Mass of Rope Below } y}\]
*Tension is perfectly linear in height \(y\). Try changing the system acceleration to negative to observe dynamic compression-like relaxation.

ODE Integration & Terminal Velocity Separable Calculus

Viscous forces are modeled as direct velocity functions. Linear regimes (\(F_d = -bv\)) govern Stokes' flow at low Reynolds numbers, while turbulent profiles (\(F_d = -cv^2\)) govern high-velocity mechanics.

Step 1: Equations of Motion & ODE Setup

Choosing downward as positive direction, the net force equation yields:

\[m \frac{dv}{dt} = mg - F_d \implies \frac{dv}{dt} = g - \frac{b}{m}v^{n}\]
Step 2: Analytical Solution (Linear \(n=1\))

Separating variables (\(v\), \(t\)) and integrating under rest boundary conditions (\(v(0) = 0\)):

\[\int_{0}^{v} \frac{dv'}{g - \frac{b}{m}v'} = \int_{0}^{t} dt' \implies v(t) = \frac{mg}{b}\left(1 - e^{-\frac{b}{m}t}\right)\]
Step 3: Terminal Equilibrium Limit

Terminal velocity \(v_{\infty}\) occurs as \(t \to \infty\), which corresponds to equilibrium (\(a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 0\)):

Linear Limit \[v_{\infty} = \frac{mg}{b}\]
Quadratic Limit \[v_{\infty} = \sqrt{\frac{mg}{c}}\]
