Constructing Newton's 2nd Law Differential Equation

Step 2 of 5

We represent the one-dimensional downward vertical motion of the mass under the force of gravity and quadratic resistance. Downward is positive for clarity:

Net Forces Formulation
Σ F = F_gravity - F_drag = m·g - β·v²

Applying Newton's second law (Σ F = m · a), and rewriting acceleration as the derivative of velocity over time (a = dv / dt):

m · (dv/dt) = m·g - β·v²
Divide through by mass (m) to isolate the derivative term:
dv/dt = g - (β/m) · v²
⚠️ This is a First-Order, NON-LINEAR Differential Equation
💡 Critical Conceptual Insight

Unlike simpler linear air resistance models, the velocity term in this ODE appears as a square (v²). This means that as speed doubles, resisting force quadruples! This rapid force surge drives the system to terminal equilibrium much quicker than linear drag models.

Applying the Method of Separation of Variables

Step 3 of 5

To solve the non-linear equation, we group all terms containing velocity (v) on the left side, and time variable (dt) on the right:

Recalling our Diff EQ dv/dt = g - (β/m)·v²

dv g - (β/m)·v²
= dt

💡 Deep Dive: Conceptual Comparison (Quadratic vs. Linear Drag)

Quadratic Drag (Current)

Applies to larger objects moving fast through normal air density (turbulent flow regime). Resistance scales with speed squared. Reaches terminal velocity quickly, stabilizing into a solid constant velocity curve.

Linear Drag

Typically models small microscopic particles moving in dense viscous fluids (laminar flow regime). Resistance is linearly proportional to speed (F_d = -b·v). Approach to terminal velocity is slower and more gradual.
