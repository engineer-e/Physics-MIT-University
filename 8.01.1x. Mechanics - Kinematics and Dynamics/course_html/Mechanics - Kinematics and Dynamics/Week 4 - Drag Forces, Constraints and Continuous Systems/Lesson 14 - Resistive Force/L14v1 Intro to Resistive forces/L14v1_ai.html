The Physics Core

Fluid drag is a resistive force opposing the relative motion of an object. Its source shifts based on how fast the object travels and how thick the fluid is:

High Speed / Inertial Drag Fd ∝ v²

Dominates at high speeds and low viscosities. The falling body actively displaces and pushes chunks of the fluid out of its path, producing a turbulent wake.

// Quadratic Drag Law:
Fd = (1/2) * Cd * ρfluid * A * v²
  • Cd: Shape drag coefficient (dimensionless)
  • A: Projected cross-sectional area (πr²)
Viscous Drag (Stokes' Law) Fd ∝ v

Dominates at micro-scales, slow speeds, or very thick (viscous) liquids. Friction between adjacent fluid layers shearing past each other generates the force.

// Linear (Stokes') Drag Law:
Fd = 6 * π * η * r * v
  • η (eta): Dynamic viscosity (resistance to shearing)
  • r: Sphere physical radius
The Boundary: Reynolds Number (Re)
Re = ρfluid · v · d / η

The Reynolds number acts as the physical scaling metric to decide which drag regime dominates:

Re << 1 (Viscous Dominated) Shearing friction governs motion. Stokes' Law is highly active.
Re >> 1000 (Inertial Dominated) Fluid displacement and pressure wake govern motion. Quadratic law active.

Deriving Terminal Velocity

At terminal velocity (v = vt), acceleration falls to zero. The force balance on the falling sphere settles into a perfect equilibrium:

Fgravity - Fbuoyancy - Fdrag = 0
1
Gravity and Mass

Mass is written as Volume (V) times Sphere Density (ρobj):

m = (4/3) π r³ ρobj
Fg = m · g
2
Buoyancy Correction (Archimedes' Principle)

An object submerged in a fluid experiences an upward force equal to the weight of displaced fluid:

Fb = Displaced Mass · g = (4/3) π r³ ρfluid g
3
Substituting into Balance Formula

Subtract buoyancy directly from gravity to get net vertical driving force:

Fdrive = (4/3) π r³ g (ρobj - ρfluid)
Resulting Analytical Terminal Velocity (vt)
Quadratic Drag Limit: High Speed
vt = √[ (8/3) · g · r · (ρobj - ρfluid) / (Cd · ρfluid) ]

Terminal velocity scales as the square root of the radius. Larger, heavier spheres fall exponentially faster.

Linear (Stokes) Drag Limit: Slow Speed
vt = [ 2 · g · r² · (ρobj - ρfluid) ] / (9 · η)

Terminal velocity scales as the square of the radius. Tiny variations in particle radius yield drastic changes in sinking rates.

Experimental Analysis: Mind Lab

Why doesn't heavy dust drop instantly?

A grain of rock dust (ρ = 2500 kg/m³) is highly dense compared to air. Yet, because its radius (r) is incredibly small, Stokes' Law applies. Viscous drag quickly matches its tiny weight, halting acceleration at terminal velocities of mere millimeters per second.

Empirical Coefficient Determination

Fluid laws are highly complex and often determined empirically. By letting spheres fall through liquids and measuring terminal velocity (vt) through sensor frames, physicists reverse-engineer fluid dynamic viscosity (η) or shape drag coefficients (Cd) accurately.
