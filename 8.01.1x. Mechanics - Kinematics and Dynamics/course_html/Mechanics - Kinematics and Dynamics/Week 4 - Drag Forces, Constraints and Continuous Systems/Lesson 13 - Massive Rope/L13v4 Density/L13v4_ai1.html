How a Computer Thinks: Riemann Sum

A computer divides geometry into discrete segments, measures the density at the center of each segment, calculates its mass, and sums them up recursively.

Step 1: Divide into finite elements (Δx, ΔA, or ΔV)
Σ dmi = Σ ρ(xi) · ΔVi

How Calculus Thinks: Exact Integral

Calculus makes the elements infinitesimally small (dx → 0), summing up infinite elements flawlessly to get the mathematically exact value.

Step 2: Take limit as ΔV → 0 to yield exact integral
∫ dm = ∫ ρ(x) · dV

Deep Dive: Density Elements Infographics

Linear Systems (1D)

λ
  • Used for thin rods, wires, strings, or beams where width/height are negligible.
  • Mass Element: dm = λ(x) · dx
  • Units: kg/m (mass per unit length)
λ = dm dx

Surface Systems (2D)

σ
  • Used for sheets, metal plates, thin discs, membranes, or surface boundaries.
  • Mass Element: dm = σ(x,y) · dA where dA = dx dy
  • Units: kg/m² (mass per unit area)
σ = dm dA

Volume Systems (3D)

ρ
  • Used for full solid geometries, spheres, cylinders, structures, planets, or gas clouds.
  • Mass Element: dm = ρ(x,y,z) · dV where dV = dx dy dz
  • Units: kg/m³ (mass per unit volume)
ρ = dm dV
