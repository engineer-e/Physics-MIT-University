Core Physics Formulation

Position Vector
$$\vec{\mathbf{r}}(t) = x(t)\hat{\mathbf{i}}$$

Specifies where the runner is at any time $t$ relative to the origin $O$ along the $\hat{\mathbf{i}}$ unit axis.

Instantaneous Velocity
$$\vec{\mathbf{v}}(t) = v(t)\hat{\mathbf{i}} = \frac{dx}{dt}\hat{\mathbf{i}}$$

The rate of change of position in time. Geometrically, this is the slope of the tangent of position curve $x(t)$.

Instantaneous Acceleration
$$\vec{\mathbf{a}}(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta \vec{\mathbf{v}}}{\Delta t} = \frac{dv_x}{dt}\hat{\mathbf{i}}$$

The limit of average acceleration as $\Delta t$ approaches $0$. This yields the exact rate of speed change at instant $t$.

Core Physics Takeaways

  • Limit Concept: Calculating instantaneous quantities is fundamentally an operation of shrinking the observation window ($\Delta t \to 0$).
  • Vector Notation: The $\hat{\mathbf{i}}$ represents a unit vector pointing along the positive x-axis, declaring our motion is purely one-dimensional.
  • Slope on Graph: Acceleration is literally the slope of the velocity curve at any single point. When velocity peaks, acceleration is exactly zero.
