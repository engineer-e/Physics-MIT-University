Core Theory & Analytical Derivation

1. Force Equations & Dimensional Analysis

A particle is acted upon in the vertical direction by:

  • Downward force of constant gravity: $F_{\text{gravity}} = -mg\,\hat{j}$
  • Time-varying upward force: $F_{\text{upward}} = bt\,\hat{j}$

Using Newton's Second Law along the vertical axis ($\hat{j}$ direction): $$F_{\text{net}, y} = bt - mg = m \frac{d^2y}{dt^2}$$

Part 1 Answer Explanation: Units of $b$

The magnitude of the upward force is $F = bt$. Since force is measured in Newtons ($\text{N} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$) and time $t$ is measured in seconds ($\text{s}$): $$[b] = \frac{[F]}{[t]} = \frac{\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2}{\text{s}} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^3 \quad \left(\text{or} \quad \text{N/s}\right)$$

2. Step-by-Step Computational Integration Path

We integrate the vertical acceleration equation sequentially with initial conditions $y(0) = 0$ (starting at the center) and $v_y(0) = 0$:

A
The Equation of Motion for Acceleration

$$\frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = a_y(t) = \frac{b}{m}t - g$$

B
Integrating once for Velocity ($v_y(t)$)

Since $v_y(0) = 0$: $$v_y(t) = \int_{0}^{t} \left(\frac{b}{m}\tau - g\right)d\tau = \frac{b}{2m}t^2 - gt$$

C
Integrating twice for Position ($y(t)$)

Since $y(0) = 0$ (at middle line of gap): $$y(t) = \int_{0}^{t} \left(\frac{b}{2m}\tau^2 - g\tau\right)d\tau = \frac{b}{6m}t^3 - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$$

3. The Critical Condition Algorithm (Theoretical Logic)

How do we guarantee the particle hits the top plate ($y = +h/2$) without ever touching the bottom plate ($y = -h/2$)?

Step 1: Locate the Extremum (Turning Point)

The turning point in vertical position is reached when $v_y(t) = 0$ (excluding $t=0$):

$$\frac{b}{2m}t_{\text{ex}}^2 - gt_{\text{ex}} = 0 \implies t_{\text{ex}} = \frac{2mg}{b}$$

Step 2: Evaluate the Lowest Dip Depth

Substitute $t_{\text{ex}}$ into the vertical position function to find the maximum downward dip:

$$y_{\text{dip}} = y(t_{\text{ex}}) = \frac{b}{6m}\left(\frac{2mg}{b}\right)^3 - \frac{1}{2}g\left(\frac{2mg}{b}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{3}\frac{m^2g^3}{b^2} - \frac{2m^2g^3}{b^2} = -\frac{2}{3}\frac{m^2g^3}{b^2}$$

Step 3: Define "No Bottom-Collision" Inequality

To avoid touching the bottom plate, the minimum value must be strictly higher than $-h/2$:

$$y_{\text{dip}} > -\frac{h}{2} \implies -\frac{2}{3}\frac{m^2g^3}{b^2} > -\frac{h}{2} \implies \frac{2}{3}\frac{m^2g^3}{b^2} < \frac{h}{2} \implies b^2 > \frac{4m^2g^3}{3h}$$

$$b > 2mg \sqrt{\frac{g}{3h}}$$

Recall Card 1

What physical state is occurring at $t_{eq} = \frac{mg}{b}$?

Equilibrium state of Forces!

Here, $F_{\text{up}} = mg$, making the instantaneous vertical acceleration $a_y = 0$. This is the moment of peak downward velocity before deceleration begins.

Recall Card 2

What is the shape of the trajectory along the plates?

Superimposed Linear-Cubic Path!

With constant horizontal speed $x(t) = v_0 t$, substituting $t = x/v_0$ into $y(t)$ yields: $$y(x) = \frac{b}{6m v_0^3}x^3 - \frac{g}{2 v_0^2}x^2$$ It acts as a parabola near the origin and transforms into a cubic rise far away.

Recall Card 3

Why is $[b]$ measured in $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^3$?

Dimensional Matching Constraint!

For the equation $F = bt$ to have units of Force ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$), the coefficient $b$ must be multiplied by seconds. Dividing force by seconds gives $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^3$.

Quick Reference Formulae

Vertical acceleration: $a_y(t) = \frac{b}{m}t - g$
Vertical velocity: $v_y(t) = \frac{bt^2}{2m} - gt$
Vertical position: $y(t) = \frac{bt^3}{6m} - \frac{gt^2}{2}$
Horizontal position: $x(t) = v_0 t$
