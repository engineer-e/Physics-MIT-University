Mathematical Proof & Vector Logic

Kinematics of the System

For the Apple (Free Fall Target):

Starts at coordinate $(d, h)$ with no initial velocity.

$$x_{\text{apple}}(t) = d$$ $$y_{\text{apple}}(t) = h - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$$
For the Bullet (Projectile):

Starts at launcher point $(0, s)$ with velocity vectors determined by angle $\theta_0$.

$$x_{\text{bullet}}(t) = v_0 \cdot t \cdot \cos\theta_0$$ $$y_{\text{bullet}}(t) = s + v_0 \cdot t \cdot \sin\theta_0 - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$$

The Magic of the Collision Time ($T$)

For a hit to occur, both objects must share identical vertical and horizontal positions at some moment $T$:

$$x_{\text{bullet}}(T) = x_{\text{apple}}(T) \implies v_0 T \cos\theta_0 = d \implies T = \frac{d}{v_0 \cos\theta_0}$$

Now we equate the vertical coordinates $y_{\text{bullet}}(T) = y_{\text{apple}}(T)$:

$$s + v_0 T \sin\theta_0 - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 = h - \frac{1}{2}gT^2$$

Look closely! The gravity components ($-\frac{1}{2}gT^2$) exist on both sides and cancel out perfectly!

$$s + v_0 T \sin\theta_0 = h \implies v_0 T \sin\theta_0 = h - s$$

Substitute the value of $T$ derived from the horizontal motion into this relation:

$$v_0 \left(\frac{d}{v_0 \cos\theta_0}\right) \sin\theta_0 = h - s \implies d \cdot \tan\theta_0 = h - s$$
$$\tan\theta_0 = \frac{h - s}{d}$$

This proves that the required angle $\theta_0$ is completely independent of gravity $g$ and velocity $v_0$. You must aim exactly along the geometric straight line connecting the gun and the apple!

Intuitive Vector Summary

Think of it like this: If gravity were turned off completely, a bullet fired directly at the target would hit it in a straight line. Now, turn gravity back on. Gravity pulls both the bullet and the apple downward by exactly the same amount ($\frac{1}{2}gt^2$) at every single millisecond. The bullet still intersects the target at the exact same point in time, just lower in vertical space!

Card 1 / Gravity Independence

How does the value of local gravity ($g$) change the angle we need to hit the falling apple?

It does NOT! The required angle is $\theta_0 = \arctan\left(\frac{h-s}{d}\right)$, which has no gravity variable. Whether on the Moon, Earth, or Jupiter, the aiming angle is identical!
Card 2 / Static Case Trap

What happens if we aim directly at the apple, but the apple does NOT drop?

We will miss! If the apple stays static, gravity still pulls the bullet down during its flight, causing it to drop below the apple. We would need to aim higher to hit it.
