Kinematic Phase Analysis

Phase I Powered Ascent (\(0 \le t \le t_0\))

The rocket is accelerating upwards against gravity using its internal fuel motor. Acceleration is non-uniform because thrust decays continuously with time due to the \(-t^4\) term:

\(a_y(t) = (a_0 - g) - a_0 \frac{t^4}{t_0^4}\)

Transition Engine Burnout (\(t = t_0\))

At exact burnout \(t_0\), the variables cancel beautifully. Setting \(t = t_0\) inside \(a_y(t)\) yields:

\(a_y(t_0) = (a_0 - g) - a_0(1) = -g\)
This ensures a completely continuous physical handover into ballistic free-fall!

Phase II Free-Fall (\(t > t_0\))

Once fuel is spent, the rocket is in free-fall. It continues coasting upward due to residual velocity until reaching peak height, then accelerates back down to Earth under constant gravity:

\(a_y(t) = -g\)

Step-by-Step Calculus Journey

Deriving Rocket Kinematics via Differentiation

Explore the continuous change from position to acceleration. Click the interactive highlighting nodes below to see how constants are treated during derivative steps.

1
Original Position Function

Start with defined vertical position \(y(t)\) for \(0 \le t \le t_0\)

The equation balances a primary launch constant \(a_0\) against Earth gravity \(g\), plus an asymmetric high-order decay component designed to model burning through rocket fuel.

$$y(t) = \frac{1}{2}(a_0 - g)t^2 - \frac{1}{30}\frac{a_0}{t_0^4}t^6$$
Differentiate with respect to time d/dt
2
Velocity Function

Take the First Derivative to find \(v_y(t)\)

Apply the polynomial power rule: \(\frac{d}{dt}(t^n) = n t^{n-1}\).
• The derivative of \(t^2\) is \(2t\), which cancels the \(\frac{1}{2}\) coefficient!
• The derivative of \(t^6\) is \(6t^5\), modifying the \(\frac{1}{30}\) factor to \(\frac{6}{30} = \frac{1}{5}\).

\(v_y(t) = \frac{d}{dt}y(t)\)
$$v_y(t) = (a_0 - g)t - \frac{1}{5}\frac{a_0}{t_0^4}t^5$$
Differentiate with respect to time d/dt
3
Acceleration Function

Take the Second Derivative to find \(a_y(t)\)

Differentiating velocity once more:
• The derivative of \((a_0 - g)t\) is simply the constant coefficient \((a_0 - g)\).
• The derivative of \(t^5\) is \(5t^4\), which beautifully cancels with the \(\frac{1}{5}\) fraction!

\(a_y(t) = \frac{d}{dt}v_y(t)\)
$$a_y(t) = (a_0 - g) - \frac{a_0}{t_0^4}t^4$$
The Elegant Physical Result

Burnout Check: What happens when \(t = t_0\)?

Substitute \(t_0\) into our newly derived acceleration equation. Watch how the \(a_0\) terms vanish completely, resulting in instant constant gravity:

Handover Result
\(a_y(t_0) = -g\)
This confirms the physical constraint that when the engine shuts down, the rocket immediately enters standard constant gravity free-fall!
