📝 Key Physics Frameworks

Position Vector (Vector Sum)

$$\vec{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j}$$

Combines independent coordinate functions along static unit vectors.

Velocity Vector (Time Derivative)

$$\vec{v}(t) = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = \frac{dx}{dt}\hat{i} + \frac{dy}{dt}\hat{j}$$

Velocity is the rate of change of position; calculated component-wise.

Infographic: Setup & Compute Algorithm

1

Establish the Coordinate System

Pick a static origin point \(O(0,0)\) and define two perpendicular directions (\(+x\) and \(+y\)).

2

Assign Constant Unit Vectors

Define \(\hat{i}\) (horizontal) and \(\hat{j}\) (vertical). In Cartesian systems, these vectors never change direction or magnitude, regardless of position.

3

Track Position Over Time

As the object follows its trajectory, map its position coordinate functions: \(x(t)\) and \(y(t)\).

4

Compute Kinematic Vectors

Use simple derivatives: position gives \(\vec{r}(t)\). Differentiating coordinates gives components of velocity \(\vec{v}(t)\).
