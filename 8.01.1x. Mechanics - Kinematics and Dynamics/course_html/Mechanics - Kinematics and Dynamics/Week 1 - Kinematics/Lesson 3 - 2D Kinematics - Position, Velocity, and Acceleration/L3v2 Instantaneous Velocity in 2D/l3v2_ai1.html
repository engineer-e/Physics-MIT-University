Mathematical Principles

1. Position Vector in 2D

Locates the object relative to the origin.

$$\vec{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j}$$

2. Vector Displacement

The difference between positions over a finite interval $\Delta t$:

$$\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}(t+\Delta t) - \vec{r}(t) = \Delta x\hat{i} + \Delta y\hat{j}$$

3. Tangent Velocity as $\Delta t \to 0$

Taking the limit yields the derivative vector (instantaneous velocity):

$$\vec{v}(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = v_x\hat{i} + v_y\hat{j}$$

4. Speed and Angular Direction

Breaking down magnitude and direction of the tangent vector:

$$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} \quad \text{and} \quad \theta = \arctan\left(\frac{v_y}{v_x}\right)$$

Algorithm & Computation Steps

1

Parse Parameteric Functions

Define coordinates dynamically as functions of time. Example for Projectile Motion: $x(t) = v_{0x}t$ and $y(t) = v_{0y}t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$.

2

Evaluate Finite Interval State

Calculate positions at times $t$ and $t + \Delta t$, then subtract to find the actual chord displacement vector $\Delta\vec{r}$.

3

Take Derivatives (The Tangent)

Analytically calculate derivatives $v_x = \frac{dx}{dt}$ and $v_y = \frac{dy}{dt}$ to establish the instant velocity vector, establishing the tangent line.

4

Polar Synthesis

Apply Pythagorean Theorem to retrieve scalar speed $v$ and Trigonometric Inverse Tangent to formulate direction angle $\theta$.

Key Physical Takeaways

As explained in the lecture transcript, the displacement vector $\Delta \vec{r}$ connects two spatial points separated by a temporal interval $\Delta t$. When we take the mathematical limit $\Delta t \to 0$, three extraordinary developments unfold:

  • ✔ Directional Shift: The vector shifts from intersecting the path at two separate coordinates (secant) to skimming exactly past the target point (tangent).
  • ✔ Decoupling Dimension: The vector analysis reveals that 2D movements are simply the sum of individual coordinate adjustments in orthogonal $x$ and $y$ axes.
  • ✔ True Instaneity: Magnitude represents actual speed, while tracking components isolates spatial changes relative to $x$ and $y$ vectors respectively.
