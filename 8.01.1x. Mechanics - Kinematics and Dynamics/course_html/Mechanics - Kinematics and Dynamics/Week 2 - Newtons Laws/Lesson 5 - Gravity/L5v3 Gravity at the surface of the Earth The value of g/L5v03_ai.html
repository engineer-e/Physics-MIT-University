Foundational Physics Equations

2. Core Formulas

This section establishes the three foundational mathematical frameworks used in classical mechanics to calculate gravity. Understanding how these variables link together is critical for the subsequent algebraic derivation and computation stages.

Universal Gravitation

The attractive force between any two masses in the universe.

F = G(m₁m₂)/r²
  • G: Gravitational Constant
  • m₁, m₂: The two masses
  • r: Distance between centers

Local Weight

The downward force experienced by an object near a planetary surface.

F = mg
  • m: Mass of the object
  • g: Local gravitational acceleration

Derived Gravity (g)

The resulting acceleration field generated by a celestial body.

g = G(M)/r²
  • M: Mass of the planet
  • r: Distance from planet center
Algebraic Breakdown

3. Interactive Algebra Derivation

How do we arrive at the formula for $g$? This section breaks down the algebraic logic steps taken on the chalkboard to derive the local gravity formula from universal principles. Click through the steps to see how the mathematical properties resolve, demonstrating why objects of different masses fall at the same rate.

1

Define the Universal Force

Identify the gravitational force between the Earth (M_E) and a test mass (m) located near its surface. The distance between their centers is approximately the radius of the Earth (R_E).

F_gravity = G(m * M_E) / R_E²
2

Define the Local Force (Weight)

Identify the standard physics equation for the weight of an object falling near the surface.

F_weight = m * g_earth
3

Equate the Forces

Since both formulas describe the exact same physical force acting on the test mass, set them equal to each other.

m * g_earth = G(m * M_E) / R_E²
4

Cancel the Test Mass

Notice that the mass of the test object (m) appears on both sides of the equation. Divide both sides by m to cancel it out.

m * g_earth = G(m * M_E) / R_E²
↓
g_earth = G(M_E) / R_E²
5

Conclude

The gravitational acceleration g is independent of the falling object's mass. It depends only on the mass of the planet, the radius of the planet, and the universal gravitational constant G.
