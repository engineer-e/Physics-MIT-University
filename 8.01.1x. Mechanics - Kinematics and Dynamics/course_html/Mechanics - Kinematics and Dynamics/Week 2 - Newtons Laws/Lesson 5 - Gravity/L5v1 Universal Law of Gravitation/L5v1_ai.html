1. The Coordinate & Position System

To describe locations unambiguously, we place an arbitrary origin point $\vec{O}$. The coordinates of the interacting bodies are defined with reference to this origin as:

• Sun (Mass $m_1$): Position Vector $\vec{r}_1$

• Earth (Mass $m_2$): Position Vector $\vec{r}_2$

• Displacement Vector: $\vec{r}_{12} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1$

This displacement vector points from $m_1$ towards $m_2$. Its length is defined as the magnitude $|\vec{r}_{12}|$.

2. Unit Vectors & Directional Math

A unit vector designates direction alone without changing magnitude. Its length is exactly 1 unit, computed by dividing the vector by its scalar magnitude:

$$\hat{r}_{12} = \frac{\vec{r}_{12}}{|\vec{r}_{12}|}$$

Because of their geometry, the unit vector pointing from 2 to 1 is the exact inverse of 1 to 2:

$$\hat{r}_{12} = -\hat{r}_{21}$$

3. Force Vector Formulation

Gravity is always attractive. Thus, the force vector acts in the opposite direction of the outward radial unit vectors:

Force on 2 due to 1: $$\vec{F}_{12} = - \frac{G \cdot m_1 \cdot m_2}{r_{12}^2} \hat{r}_{12}$$
Force on 1 due to 2: $$\vec{F}_{21} = - \frac{G \cdot m_1 \cdot m_2}{r_{12}^2} \hat{r}_{21}$$

Because $\hat{r}_{12} = -\hat{r}_{21}$, substituting gives the foundational truth of Newton's Third Law: $$\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}$$

Mathematical Summary of Gravitational Vector Theory

Law

Universal Law of Gravitation (Vector Form)

$$\vec{F}_{12} = -G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_{12}^2} \hat{r}_{12}$$ Where $G \approx 6.67430 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}^3\text{kg}^{-1}\text{s}^{-2}$, $m_1$ and $m_2$ are masses, $r_{12}$ is the distance between them, and $\hat{r}_{12}$ is the radial unit vector.

Direction

Significance of the Negative Sign (-)

The displacement vector $\vec{r}_{12}$ and its unit vector $\hat{r}_{12}$ point outward (from the source body $m_1$ to the affected body $m_2$). Because gravity is an attractive force pulling $m_2$ back toward $m_1$, the force vector $\vec{F}_{12}$ must point in the exact opposite direction. This is mathematically dictated by the negative sign.

Equality

Newton's Third Law (Action-Reaction)

$$\vec{F}_{12} = - \vec{F}_{21}$$ The force exerted by the Sun on the Earth ($\vec{F}_{12}$) is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force exerted by the Earth on the Sun ($\vec{F}_{21}$). There is no asymmetry; both bodies pull on each other with the exact same amount of force.

Physics Fact Sheet

  • Central Force: Gravitational force acts directly along the line of centers connecting the two interacting masses.
  • Inverse Square Nature: Double the distance between objects, and the gravitational pull is divided by exactly 4 ($2^2$).
  • Polar Coordinates Preference: Planetary and orbital trajectories are far easier to model using polar vectors ($r, \theta$) instead of standard $(x, y)$ coordinate grids.
  • Infinite Range: Gravity reaches across the entire universe. While the force becomes infinitesimal over massive distances, it never technically falls to zero.

Vector Calculation Sequence

Below is the precise mathematical sequence used by our simulation engine to dynamically compute vector lengths, directions, and force properties:

1

Extract Position Coordinates

Read positions from origin: $\vec{r}_1 = [x_1, y_1]$ and $\vec{r}_2 = [x_2, y_2]$.

2

Compute Displacement Vector ($\vec{r}_{12}$)

$\vec{r}_{12} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1 = [x_2 - x_1, y_2 - y_1]$

3

Calculate Absolute Distance ($r_{12}$)

$r_{12} = |\vec{r}_{12}| = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$

4

Derive Normalized Unit Vectors ($\hat{r}_{12}, \hat{r}_{21}$)

$\hat{r}_{12} = \left[ \frac{x_2-x_1}{r_{12}}, \frac{y_2-y_1}{r_{12}} \right]$,    $\hat{r}_{21} = -\hat{r}_{12}$

5

Resolve Force Magnitudes and Vectors

Scalar magnitude $F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_{12}^2}$
Vector representation: $\vec{F}_{12} = -F \cdot \hat{r}_{12}$  and  $\vec{F}_{21} = -F \cdot \hat{r}_{21}$

Simulation Engine Code Logic

Here is the JavaScript implementation used to keep state and calculate physical force vectors dynamically under the hood inside our rendering loop: 

// 1. Read coordinates and system parameters
const dx = earth.x - sun.x;
const dy = earth.y - sun.y;

// 2. Compute distance
const dist = Math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy);
const distSq = dist * dist;

// 3. Compute Unit Vectors
const r12_hat_x = dx / dist;
const r12_hat_y = dy / dist;

const r21_hat_x = -r12_hat_x;
const r21_hat_y = -r12_hat_y;

// 4. Calculate Force Magnitudes (scaled)
const forceMag = (G * m1 * m2) / distSq;

// 5. Map force direction against unit vectors
const F12_x = -forceMag * r12_hat_x;
const F12_y = -forceMag * r12_hat_y;

const F21_x = -forceMag * r21_hat_x;
const F21_y = -forceMag * r21_hat_y;
