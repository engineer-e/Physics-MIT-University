Newton's Third Law Pairs

Forces always occur in equal and opposite interaction pairs. Each pair involves the exact same two objects interacting.

Identify the Interaction Pairs:

PAIR 1: Contact Normal Force

Block 1 pushes upward on Block 2 to support its weight (N₁₂). Simultaneously, Block 2 pushes down on Block 1 with an equal magnitude force (N₂₁).

N₁₂ = N₂₁ = m₂ · g
PAIR 2: Inter-block Friction

When Block 1 is pushed right, Block 2 has a tendency to slide backward relative to Block 1. Block 1 exerts static/kinetic friction forward on Block 2 (f₁₂), while Block 2 exerts an equal and opposite friction backward on Block 1 (f₂₁).

f₁₂ = f₂₁ ≤ μs · m₂ · g

Note: Gravity and Ground Normal are not action-reaction pairs because they act on the same block. Ground Normal's reaction partner is Block 1 pushing down on the earth!

Slip Threshold Derivation

How do we calculate the absolute maximum force Fmax we can apply before slipping occurs? Let's walk through the math.

Step 1: Maximum acceleration of Block 2

Block 2 is only accelerated by the friction force from Block 1. The maximum possible static friction force is:

fs,max = μs · m₂ · g

Applying Newton's 2nd Law for Block 2:

amax = fs,max / m₂ = μs · g
Step 2: No-slip unified motion condition

To avoid slipping, the acceleration of the entire combined system cannot exceed amax.

Considering System C (Both Blocks):

F - fk,ground = (m₁ + m₂) · a

Where ground friction is kinetic: fk,ground = μg · (m₁ + m₂) · g.

Step 3: Solve for Maximum Force

Set a = amax and substitute back:

Fmax = (m₁ + m₂) · g · (μs + μg)
