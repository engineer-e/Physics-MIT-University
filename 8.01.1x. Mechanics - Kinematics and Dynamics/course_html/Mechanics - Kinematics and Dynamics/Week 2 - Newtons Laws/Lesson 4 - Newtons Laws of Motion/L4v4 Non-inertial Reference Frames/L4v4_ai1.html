Frame S (Inertial)

The Ground Observer

Velocity $V$ is constant or zero. Relative acceleration $A = 0$. Coordinates use absolute vectors $\vec{r}$. Newton's laws hold natively without correction factors.

Acceleration: a F_net = m·a
Frame S' (Non-Inertial)

The Moving Observer

The frame has relative displacement $\vec{R}$ and acceleration $\vec{A}$. An observer inside registers relative coordinate vector $\vec{r}' = \vec{r} - \vec{R}$.

Acceleration: a' = a - A F' = m·a'
Fictitious Force

Apparent Forces

An artifact of coordinate shifting. No physical agent is pushing. Quantified as $F_{\text{fict}} = -mA$ to correct frame acceleration.

Correction Vector F_fictitious = -m·A

Fictitious Vector Math & Computational Blueprint

Step-by-step algorithms used by theoretical physicists to solve systems inside non-inertial frames.

Analytical & Physical Algorithm

1

Map the Position Coordinate Vectors

Establish origin vectors: $\vec{r} = \vec{R} + \vec{r}'$ where $\vec{R}$ tracks S' origin coordinates, and $\vec{r}'$ tracks the object relative to S'.

2

Establish the Frame Acceleration Vector

Define the vector acceleration of the non-inertial frame $S'$ relative to S. In this linear cart simulation, $\vec{A} = \frac{d^2\vec{R}}{dt^2} = A\hat{i}$.

3

Construct the Fictitious Component

Introduce the coordinate correction term: $\vec{F}_{\text{fictitious}} = -m\vec{A}$. It acts as an apparent force pushing opposite to the frame's acceleration direction.

4

Solve in Non-Inertial Coordinates

Apply Newton's Second Law for S': $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}' = \vec{F}_T + \vec{F}_g + \vec{F}_{\text{fict}} = m\vec{a}'$. At equilibrium, $\vec{a}' = 0$, giving $\theta_{\text{eq}} = \arctan(A/g)$.

Rotating Frames: Centrifugal & Coriolis

If frame S' is rotating with angular velocity $\vec{\omega}$ relative to S, two famous fictitious forces arise: $\vec{F}_{\text{Centrifugal}} = -m \vec{\omega} \times (\vec{\omega} \times \vec{r}')$
$\vec{F}_{\text{Coriolis}} = -2m (\vec{\omega} \times \vec{v}')$ These forces dictate major systems like atmospheric cyclones and space station artificial gravity chambers.

The Equivalence Principle

Notice how the fictitious force $\vec{F}_{\text{fict}} = -m\vec{A}$ scales *exactly* with mass. This means it accelerates all bodies identically, regardless of composition. Einstein realized this matches gravity perfectly! This insight—the **Equivalence Principle**—established that gravitation is indistinguishable from coordinate acceleration, forming the bedrock of **General Relativity**.
