Newtonian Mechanics at a Glance

Understanding the core framework that defines our physical world, structured from the principles of isolation to variable mass systems.

Isolated Bodies

An isolated body is one experiencing a net physical external force of exactly $0$. Distanced from all interactions, it persists infinitely in its state of constant straight-line motion.

Inertial Reference Frames

A coordinate system where Newton's First Law holds true. In an inertial frame, if the net force acting on an object is zero, its acceleration is zero. Non-inertial frames involve physical acceleration without real external forces.

Change of Mass ($dp/dt$)

While $F = ma$ is perfectly valid for standard point masses with static inertia, variable mass systems (like rocket propulsion or mass flow streams) must be evaluated via the time derivative of momentum: $F_{net} = \frac{dp}{dt}$.

The Mechanical Decision Tree

SCENARIO A

The Body is Isolated ($\Sigma F = 0$)

If no outside interactions act on a body, it remains physically undisturbed. What happens next depends strictly on the observer's coordinate choice:

  • 1. Inertial Frame: Body moves at constant velocity $v$ (which might be $0$). Acceleration $a = 0$.
  • 2. Non-Inertial Frame: Coordinate system accelerates at $a_{frame}$. An observer perceives the body accelerating at $-a_{frame}$ despite zero actual physical force applying.
SCENARIO B

The Body is Not Isolated ($\Sigma F \neq 0$)

Physical contact or fields exert an external net force on the entity. The system accelerates proportionally:

  • 1. Constant Mass: Under standard point mass circumstances where mass is immutable ($dm/dt = 0$), Newton's Second Law is calculated via: $F = ma$.
  • 2. Flowing Mass: In situations where mass is dynamic or flowing (exhaust fuel, fluid streams), momentum changes must be evaluated using: $F = \frac{dp}{dt}$.

Mathematics & Integration Algorithms

A physics simulator performs discrete state updates using mathematical integration rules. Here we expose the algorithms driving our interactive simulation environment.

Point Mass Momentum
$$p = m \cdot v$$
Momentum is mass times the instantaneous velocity. For isolated bodies, momentum is conserved.
General Second Law
$$F = \frac{dp}{dt}$$
The applied force equals the rate of change of momentum. When mass fluctuates, it creates a thrust/drag term.
Variable Rocket Motion
$$m \frac{dv}{dt} = F_{ext} + u_e \frac{dm}{dt}$$
Rocket equation modeling thrust output. Ejected fuel particles transport momentum in the opposing direction.

Step-by-Step Computational Algorithm Steps

1
Determine Total State and External Forces

Every frame ($dt \approx 0.016\text{s}$), calculate the sum of all forces: $F_{net} = F_{applied} + F_{friction} + F_{pseudoforce}$

2
Perform Dynamic Mass Update (For Variable Mass Systems)

If rocket combustion is engaged, fuel is burned: $m_{new} = m_{old} - \frac{dm}{dt} \cdot dt$ We must cap mass updates to prevent negative mass states.

3
Calculate Acceleration & Integrate Velocity (Euler-Cromer Method)

Acceleration is derived directly from the current mass-telemetry and forces: $a = \frac{F_{net} + u_e \cdot \frac{dm}{dt}}{m}$ Velocity is updated iteratively using this acceleration: $v_{new} = v_{old} + a \cdot dt$

4
Integrate Position

Position is advanced using the integrated velocity vector: $x_{new} = x_{old} + v_{new} \cdot dt$
