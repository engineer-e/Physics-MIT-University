The Prandtl-Tomlinson Model

Formulated independently by Prandtl (1928) and Tomlinson (1929), this model represents the friction on a single-atom tip connected to a support moving with velocity v via a spring k.

Mathematical Potential:
V(x, t) = -V₀·cos(2π·x / a) + ½·k·(x - v·t)²
The Friction Transition: If the spring parameter η = (2π/a)² (V₀/k) < 1, only a single minimum exists, and the sliding is continuous and completely frictionless. If η > 1, multi-stability occurs, creating sudden energy-dissipating slips (heat production).

Superlubricity & Golden Ratio

When multiple atoms contact a substrate, the relationship between their spacing (d) and the substrate lattice constant (a) governs the friction.

Commensurate (d = a) All atoms sit in energy minima together. They stick and slip synchronously, producing high friction.
Incommensurate (d / a = φ) Atoms sit in mismatched locations. Barriers cancel out, producing almost zero friction (Superlubricity).
The Aubry Transition: When the chain matches the mathematical Golden Ratio (φ = 1.618033...), it represents the most incommensurate scenario possible, exhibiting remarkable sliding behavior.

Thermolubricity & Velocity Regimes

At non-zero temperatures (T > 0), atomic kinetic energy fluctuates. This thermal activation helps the atom "hop" over energy barriers before they completely vanish, significantly reducing net friction.

Computational Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE):
m·ẍ + γ·ẋ = -∂V/∂x + η_thermal(t)
<η_thermal(t) η_thermal(t')> = 2γ·k_B·T·δ(t-t')
The Velocity Regimes: At ultra-low speeds, thermal fluctuations completely dominate, leading to continuous hopping and vanishing friction. As speed rises, the system shifts through a logarithmic increase towards flat temperature-independent macroscopic behavior.

Nanophysical Theory & Algorithmic Design

The Prandtl-Tomlinson Potential

To compute the instantaneous physical forces, we compute the gradient of the spatial system potential:

F_total = -∂V/∂x = -V₀(2π/a)sin(2πx/a) - k(x - vt)

The dynamics are integrated via a robust Leapfrog/Euler scheme, ensuring full conservation of energy during elastic deformation phases.

Multi-Atom Chain (Frenkel-Kontorova)

For an array of particles connected by mutual spring forces:

F_chain_i = k_chain·(x_i+1 - 2x_i + x_i-1)

We resolve coupling interactions alongside substrate constraints. When structural spacing is mismatched (e.g., using golden ratio offsets), phase-shifting forces cancel spatial barriers, leading to vanishing static friction.

Stochastic Euler-Maruyama Solver

To replicate realistic temperature fluctuations ($T > 0$), we inject Gaussian white noise:

dx_i = v_i·dt dv_i = [-γv_i - ∂V/∂x]dt + dW(t)

Where dW is derived from random Gaussian sampling scaled directly by sqrt(2·γ·k_B·T·dt), reproducing Brownian hopping.
