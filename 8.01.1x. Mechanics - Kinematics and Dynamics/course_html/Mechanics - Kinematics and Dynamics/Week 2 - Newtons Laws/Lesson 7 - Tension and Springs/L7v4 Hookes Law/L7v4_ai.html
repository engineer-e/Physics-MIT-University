Hooke's Law Step-By-Step Masterclass

An idealized spring acts as a source of a linear restoring force. The physical behavior is governed by Hooke's Law:

  • $x$: The extension (for $x > 0$) or compression (for $x < 0$) from the equilibrium position ($x = 0$).
  • The Negative Sign ($-k$): Acts as a restoring agent. It pulls the mass in the direction opposite to its displacement, always returning it to equilibrium.
  • Spring Constant $k$: Measures the stiffness of the spring. Measured in Newtons per meter (N/m).

Observe the force vector arrows in the live viewport. When you pull the block right (positive), the force arrow points left. When you compress it left, the arrow reverses instantly!

By combining Hooke's Law with Newton's Second Law of Motion ($\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$), we construct the governing equation of motion:

The solution to this homogeneous second-order linear differential equation is a pure sinusoidal wave with natural frequency $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$:

$x(t) = A \cos(\omega_0 t + \phi)$

This forms the foundation of all harmonic motion. If we introduce dampening/friction, a damping force term $-b \dot{x}$ is added, transitioning the path from a perfect cyclic orbit to a decaying spiral!

Computers cannot solve continuous differential equations directly; instead, they discretize time into tiny increments $dt$ and solve them step-by-step. Let's compare our options:

1. Explicit Euler (Linear Approximation)

Calculates next position using *current* velocity. It adds non-physical energy to the system at every timestep, causing the simulation to blow up if $dt$ is large.

2. Verlet Integration (Symplectic)

Tracks momentum conservation. It is highly stable for physical systems because it preserves total mechanical energy ($E_{tot} = K + U$) perfectly over time.

3. Runge-Kutta 4th Order (RK4)

Samples four distinct derivatives across the timestep interval to find a weighted average trajectory. The gold standard for precision.
