What this Simulator Proves (Video Correlation)

1

Independence of Contact Area ($A$): Switch between Wide ($A_1$) and Tall ($A_2$) orientations. Note that even though the distribution over the surface changes dramatically, the calculated kinetic friction force ($f^k$) remains completely identical!

2

Independence of Velocity ($v$): Run the simulator at different speeds. Notice that once the object is in motion, the friction vector ($f^k$) doesn't scale with velocity. It only depends on the normal force and surface coefficient.

3

The Balance of Forces ($F = f^k$): In order to maintain a constant velocity ($\vec{v} = \text{constant}$), your applied force must perfectly equal the kinetic friction force, leading to a net acceleration of $a = 0$.

Core Physics Formula

Mathematical Infographic

The Friction Law of Amontons-Coulomb
$$|{\vec{f}}^k| = \mu_k |\vec{N}|$$

The magnitude of kinetic friction force is directly proportional to the magnitude of the normal force, scaled by the coefficient of friction.

Component Analysis

f^k
Kinetic Friction Force (${\vec{f}}^k$) Tangential opposing force acting parallel to the contact interface, opposite to relative motion.
\mu_k
Coefficient of Kinetic Friction ($\mu_k$) A dimensionless constant based entirely on the materials in contact. Independent of area and speed.
N
Normal Force ($\vec{N}$) The perpendicular contact force exerted by the surface onto the object (often equal to $mg$ on a flat plane).
C
Total Contact Force ($\vec{C}$) The true vector sum of normal and tangential components: $\vec{C} = \vec{N} + {\vec{f}}^k$
Sim Computational Steps
  1. Calculate Gravity: $W = m \cdot g$ (where $g = 9.81 \, \text{m/s}^2$).
  2. Resolve Normal Force: On a horizontal plane, $N = W$.
  3. Compute Friction Limit: $f^k = \mu_k \cdot N$.
  4. Determine Motion State: If $F > f^k$ (or if currently moving), net acceleration is computed using Newton's 2nd Law:
    $a = \frac{F - f^k}{m}$
  5. Update System Kinematics: Apply integration step: $v_{t+1} = v_t + a \cdot \Delta t$ $x_{t+1} = x_t + v \cdot \Delta t$
